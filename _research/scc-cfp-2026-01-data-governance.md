## 2. Likely rules, inferred from past SCC calls

### 2.1 Closest precedent: CFP-2025-07, "Advancing Artificial Intelligence through Standardization Strategies and Tools"

Posted 2025-09-05 and closed 2025-10-06, 14:00. The same ISED-funded program runs both calls. Sources: [CFP-2025-07 PDF](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/sites/default/files/webform/tender_notice/73635/scc_cfp_2025-07-main-document_v.1_2025-09-05_en.pdf), [MERX abstract](https://www.merx.com/public/supplier/solicitations/notice/22784983501/abstract).

- **Eligible deliverable types.** There were four:
  - Technical Specification (TS)
  - Publicly Available Specification (PAS)
  - Workshop Agreement (WA)
  - Community-Sourced Guidance (CSG)

  Bidders had to say which type their project fits best and why. "Table 1" set the deliverables and a **maximum funding amount per type**. The dollar amounts were not visible in the indexed text.
- **Proposal package.**
  - A Technical Proposal of **15 pages maximum, 11 pt minimum**, including a **preliminary draft Statement of Work**.
  - An Acceptance Form.
  - A Financial Template.
- **Evaluation.**
  - Stage 1 is point-rated, with a **70/100 minimum**.
  - Which proposals go on to Stage 2 is decided "based on all the submissions received", so Stage 2 is comparative.
  - Reaching Stage 2 does not guarantee funding.
- **Principles every solution had to follow.**
  - Subject-matter-expert participation, consultation, collaboration, transparency, neutrality and structure.
  - Relevant **international standards used wherever possible**.
  - Indigenous engagement expected where relevant.
- **Timing.** Work had to start within 3 months of contract signature. Early-stage projects were allowed.
- **Bilingual capacity.** A written attestation that the bidder can deliver in both official languages under the Official Languages Act.

### 2.3 SCC's standard procurement rules

From RFP 2023-22, 2025-02 and 2025-04:

- Questions go **in writing to contracts@scc.ca** before a published cut-off (for example, 14:00 EST about 2 weeks before closing).
- Answers are published to all bidders as **CanadaBuys amendments**.
- Late proposals are refused.
- The evaluation committee has at least 3 members.
- Evaluation runs in phases: mandatory requirements first, then point-rated criteria (similar past work, project plan, information-management plan).

Sources:
- [RFP 2023-22](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/sites/default/files/webform/tender_notice/23736/scc_rfp_2023-22-pas-equity-lens-infrastructure_v1_2023-11-01.pdf), a single PAS project with the budget **capped at $150,000 before tax**

### 2.4 What CFP-2026-01 probably looks like (inference)

1. The same template as CFP-2025-07:
   - a Technical Proposal of about 15 pages with a draft Statement of Work
   - an Acceptance Form
   - a Financial Template
   - a bilingual attestation
2. A choice of deliverable type (TS, PAS, WA or CSG), each with its own funding cap.
   - A plausible cap is **$150K–$300K per project** over 18 months. **No figure is confirmed.**
3. Each proposal is mapped to one Section 3.2 area and to specific numbered roadmap recommendations.
4. A 70% technical threshold, then highest merit within the maximum budget.
5. Unknown until the document is read:
   - who can bid: whether an accredited SDO is required, and whether consortia and a Canadian legal entity are allowed or required
   - IP and copyright of the deliverable (SCC calls often need an SDO to publish it)
   - payment milestones
   - reporting
   - insurance and security clauses

SCC's descriptions of the deliverable types:
- [Overview](https://scc-ccn.ca/standards/flexible-standards-based-solutions)
- [Workshop Agreement](https://scc-ccn.ca/standards/flexible-standards-based-solutions/workshop-agreement): published within about 2 months of the workshop
- [PAS](https://scc-ccn.ca/standards/flexible-standards-based-solutions/publicly-available-specification): sponsor-driven, reviewed after 3 years
- [Community-Sourced Guidance](https://scc-ccn.ca/standards/flexible-standards-based-solutions/community-sourced-guidance): does not need full consensus

## 3. The 2021 DGSC Roadmap (the scope)

- **Title and publication.**
  - *Canadian Data Governance Standardization Roadmap*, SCC, June 2021.
  - GC Publications catalogue no. Iu81-3/18-2021E-PDF.
  - [Landing page](https://scc-ccn.ca/resources/publications/canadian-data-governance-standardization-roadmap), [PDF](https://scc-ccn.ca/system/files/2024-05/scc_data_gov_roadmap_en.pdf), [ISED launch release](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2021/06/minister-champagne-marks-the-launch-of-the-canadian-data-governance-standardization-collaborative-roadmap.html).
- **Who wrote it.** The Data Governance Standardization Collaborative, set up by SCC in 2019. It had **220 experts** from government, industry, civil society, Indigenous organizations, academia and SDOs. ([SCC case study](https://scc-ccn.ca/resources/case-studies/canadian-data-governance-collaborative-supporting-data-governance-through))
- **Content.**
  - It makes **35 recommendations** on gaps where standards or conformity assessment are needed.
  - Working groups followed the data life cycle: **collection → access and sharing → analytics and commercialization**, plus foundations. ([Global Government Forum](https://www.globalgovernmentforum.com/canada-launches-data-governance-standardisation-initiative/))
- **Cross-cutting themes.** ([librarianship.ca](https://librarianship.ca/news/data-governance-roadmap/))
  - **Quality:** standardized systems and controls.
  - **Trust:** privacy, security and transparency.
  - **Ethics:** ethical and explainable AI/ML.
- **Other known content:**
  - a section on Indigenous data sovereignty
  - use cases for open banking and consumer-directed finance
  - two AI recommendations, paraphrased in a secondary source:
    - standardize AI terminology and life-cycle components, and specifications for verification and validation
    - standardize governance approaches in organizations that use or build AI, including conformity-assessment-based standards **such as ISO/IEC 42001**
- **Issue numbering.** "Issue 5" is **data management governance**, per [Lexpert, June 2026](https://www.lexpert.ca/news/health-sciences-law/development-begins-on-data-governance-framework-for-healthcare-organizations/394319).
- **Still needed.** The full numbered list of the 35 recommendations, their issues, priorities and suggested leads. We could not get it; take it from the PDF. The CFP's Section 3.2 will name which ones are "selected".

## 6. International standards to align with

The CFP will likely require international standards "wherever possible".

- **ISO/IEC 38505-1:2026**, governance of data. 2nd edition, August 2026, from JTC 1/SC 40. ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/87195.html))
- **ISO/IEC 5259-1…5**, data quality for analytics and ML. Part 5 (the governance framework) is from 2025. ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/81088.html))
- **ISO/IEC 8183:2023**, AI data life cycle framework.
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** with 42005 (impact assessment) and 42006 (certification bodies). CSA has adopted 42001 as CSA ISO/IEC 42001:25.
- **ISO/IEC 27701:2025**, now a standalone privacy information management system.
- **ISO 8000** data quality. ISO/AWI 8000-3, a management-system standard for data, is in development.
- **ISO/IEC 20889:2018** and **27559:2022**, de-identification.
- **ISO/IEC 5207 / 5212:2024**, data usage.
- **ISO/IEC 23751:2022**, data sharing agreements.
- Canadian mirror committees (SC 27, 32, 38, 40, 42) are open to members through [SCC](https://scc-ccn.ca/get-involved/become-member/join-mirror-committee). Joining one strengthens a bid's "expertise" score.

## 7. Fit for ascio and next steps

### Competitors and partners

- **Most likely competitors:**
  - DGSI / Digital Governance Council, the incumbent
  - CSA Group
  - HSO, on health topics
  - sector nonprofits bidding with an SDO (the RegTech Association, OFNC, SETSI)
  - Big-4 and assurance firms
  - the Responsible AI Institute, Armilla, Nemko Digital
- **Partners to consider:**
  - An accredited SDO to publish the deliverable. DGSI is the likeliest; CSA Group fits if the work adopts ISO/IEC standards.
  - An SCC-accredited certification body such as MHM Advisory, for conformity assessment design.
  - A sector proponent:
    - OFNC or FDX for finance
    - Canada Health Infoway, CIHI or HRSO for health
    - DIACC for trust and identity
    - Open North for public-sector data
    - an Indigenous-led partner (FNIGC or a regional centre) for any Indigenous-data element

### Where ascio could stand out (assessment)

Few bidders combine data governance with **conformity assessment and assurance** for regulated sectors. Candidate projects, to be checked against Section 3.2 once it is retrieved:

1. **A data governance conformity / assurance scheme (PAS or TS).** Auditable requirements and a maturity model that sit across ISO/IEC 38505-1:2026, 5259-5, 27701:2025 and 42001, crosswalked to CAN/DGSI 100-7 and 100-8. It would be designed for SCC-accredited certification bodies to use. This targets roadmap recommendations on conformity assessment and data management governance.
2. **Data governance for critical infrastructure operators (WA or TS).** Aligned with the Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act (C-8), ITSP.10.171 and CPCSC, covering data classification, residency and third-party data access for designated operators and defence suppliers.
3. **A data-quality and provenance specification for AI training data (TS)**, adopting ISO/IEC 5259 and 8183 for Canadian SMEs, and linked to the AIDG Hub's SME priority and the proposed "Canada Trusted AI Certification".

Avoid topics DGSI has already covered: health data governance, consent, portability, sovereignty, employee data, nonprofits and research data (see 3.1).
